1. Agencies & officials

Tariff Commission (TC)1 Chairperson + 2 Commissioners (Sec. 1600), appointed by the President. Per key (#173): under the Office of the President. In practice it is attached to NEDA, which isn't a choice there.
TC employeesAppointed by the Chairperson under Civil Service Law, except the Private Secretary to the Commissioner (Sec. 1601).
TC doesInvestigates & recommends on tariff changes, trade agreements, safeguards; administers the AHTN in PH; implements Sec. 1608; investigates safeguard petitions. Its classification rulings are binding until revoked.
Bureau of CustomsAssessment, collection, control & enforcement of customs laws. Under DOF (general supervision). Commissioner appointed by the President. BOC does not fix tariff rates.
Customs brokerLicensed by the PRC. Import entry: importer or licensed broker (#347). Export entry: exporter or licensed broker (#348).
AEOAccredited by BOC. Perks: fewer inspections, faster release, priority.
Sec. 102Jurisdictional control: BOC's supervision & police authority over seas within PH jurisdiction.
Sec. 100–101 policyNOT a policy: "unrestricted importation of all goods" or "eliminate all imports".

2. CMTA tariff sections (1600s)

1600TC officials: Chairperson + 2 Commissioners
1601TC employees (except Private Secretary to the Commissioner)

Memory hook: 08 = flexible (adjust rates), 09 = agreements, 10 = GRI rules, 12 = export (logs).

3. HS / AHTN structure

  • HS developed by the Customs Cooperation Council (now WCO). HS Convention in force 1988. GIRs are an integral part of the HS Convention.
  • 21 Sections, written in Roman numerals. Sec. I = live animals; animal products. Sec. XV = base metals. Sec. XXI = works of art, collectors' pieces, antiques.
  • Chapters: Ch. 1 live animals, Ch. 97 works of art & antiques, Ch. 77 reserved. Arranged by degree of processing.
Digits 1–2Chapter
Digits 1–4Heading (4 digits)
Digits 5–6Subheading (HS level, 6 digits)
Digits 7–8ASEAN subheading. All AMS share codes up to 8 digits.
Beyond 8National subheading. Reviewer: TAP-AHTN = 11-digit codes, digits 9–11 national (#186, #203). #133 keys "10 digits" for AHTN 2022, so know both.
  • Dashes: one dash = first level of subdivision under a heading. 8503.00.11 → three dashes.
  • Colon (:) = further subdivided. Period (.) = only the products named. Semicolon (;) = items distinct & separate.
  • "Other" = goods in the heading not covered by earlier subheadings at the same dash level.
  • Legal texts: headings, subheadings, Section/Chapter notes. Explanatory Notes are NOT legal text (official commentary, persuasive). Legal texts do not give duty rates.
  • SEN (Supplementary Explanatory Notes): define goods unique to ASEAN; help interpret ASEAN subheadings; previously in TCCP-AHTN Vol. I.
  • AHTN Protocol ("Protocol Governing the Implementation of the AHTN"): all AMS use AHTN to 8 digits; may add national subheadings beyond 8; monitored by the ASEAN Secretariat. FALSE: "AHTN is only for intra-ASEAN trade".
  • TAP-AHTN 2017: used for ATIGA, ACFTA, PJEPA (all). Carries MFN and CEPT rates.
  • First step in classifying: identify the goods (what, characteristics, function, condition).

4. General Interpretative Rules (GIRs)

GIR 1Titles are for reference only. Classify by the terms of the headings + Section/Chapter Notes.
GIR 2(a)Incomplete/unfinished article with the essential character = the finished article. Also unassembled/disassembled (CKD) articles.
GIR 2(b)Mixtures & combinations of materials.
GIR 3(a)Most specific description wins. If headings each cover only part of a composite → treated as equally specific.
GIR 3(b)Essential character: mixtures, composites, sets for retail sale (e.g., knife-fork-spoon set; 60% wool / 40% cotton).
GIR 3(c)Heading that occurs last in numerical order.
GIR 4Heading for goods most akin.
GIR 5Cases, containers, packing. 5(a): fitted cases sold with the article (camera + case together). 5(b): packing goes with the goods unless clearly reusable. GIR 5 is the "different" one (#54).
GIR 6Subheading level classification.

Key examples: multi-function machine → Rule 3 (#6). Unassembled article → 2(a) (#5). CKD 50cc motorcycle → 2(a) and 6 (#34). "Highest value" is not a GIR principle.

5. Customs valuation (CMTA Sec. 700–706)

M1 · Sec. 701Transaction Value: price actually paid or payable, adjusted.
M2 · Sec. 702Identical goods: same in all respects (minor appearance differences OK), same producer, exported at or about the same time (~90 days). Several values → use the lowest.
M3 · Sec. 703Similar goods: similar characteristics & commercially interchangeable.
M4 · Sec. 704Deductive: unit price at the first sale after importation in the greatest aggregate quantity, sold in the same condition. Deduct commissions/profit & general expenses, transport, PH duties & taxes. Not deducted: exporter's profit.
M5 · Sec. 705Computed: materials + production costs + profit & general expenses + transport to port of export. Excludes PH customs duties.
M6 · Sec. 706Fallback / Residual: reasonable means consistent with WTO principles; must NOT be based on the prohibited bases ("all of the above").
  • Order: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6. Exception: importer may ask to reverse Methods 4 and 5, declared at the time of the import declaration and if customs doesn't object.
  • Per key: value set >45 days from B/L date → Method Six (#3). Answer #3 by its own key, even though #144 and #272 say "at or about the same time" is ~90 days. Exception to sequential rule worded as "Method Four to Method Six" (#20).
  • Dutiable value = CIF. Example: FOB 1,000 + freight 150 + insurance 50 = USD 1,200.

✅ Add (dutiable)

  • Selling commissions
  • Packing & containers
  • Assists (supplied free or at reduced cost)
  • Royalties/license fees related to the goods & a condition of sale
  • Proceeds of resale that accrue to the seller
  • Freight & insurance to the PH port of entry

❌ Don't add

  • Buying commissions
  • Post-importation transport
  • Post-importation construction, assembly, maintenance
  • Interest on deferred payment (separately shown, written arrangement)
  • PH customs duties & taxes
  • Discounts actually granted at importation
  • TV is OK despite restrictions imposed by PH law/authorities, geographic resale limits, or ones not substantially affecting value. Not OK: buyer must buy other goods at an inflated price.
  • Consignment = not a sale → can't use Method 1.
  • Related parties: 8 instances. A sole agent/distributor is not automatically related. Distant cousins beyond the 4th civil degree are not related. Related sale OK if the relationship didn't influence the price or TV closely approximates a test value.
  • Pro forma invoice is never TV evidence; the commercial invoice is.
  • Price actually paid or payable = total payments made or to be made, directly or indirectly, by the buyer to or for the seller (#117, #139, #258).
  • TV assumes a sale for export between unrelated parties with no restrictive conditions, e.g. a direct sale from a foreign seller to a PH buyer (#140, #278). Seller keeping title until payment does not bar Method 1 (#279). Related parties don't automatically bar TV (#136).
  • Related-party test values: TV of identical goods to unrelated buyers, or customs values of identical/similar goods. Not a test value: "the invoice price from any supplier worldwide" (#137).

7. Rules of Origin

  • ROO = principles that determine a good's country of origin (its "economic nationality").
  • Non-preferential: MFN treatment, trade statistics, procurement. Preferential: FTA tariff concessions.
  • Wholly obtained: produced entirely in one country, no foreign materials.
  • CTC applies only to non-originating materials. CTH = change at heading (4-digit); CTSH = subheading.
  • RVC = % value added in the region. ATIGA needs ≥ 40%.
  • De minimis: small % of non-originating materials ignored. Accumulation: combine value from member countries.
  • Back-to-back CO: trader in a member country re-exports to another member. Blanket CO: many shipments of identical goods, up to 1 year.
  • Direct consignment: ship directly, or stay under customs control when transiting a non-member.
  • ATIGA general rule: WO or RVC ≥ 40% or CTH (#313's key adds CTSH; still pick it). Specific rules = product-specific (RVC, CTC or process).

8. ASEAN, FTAs & trade groups

ATIGAASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement · CO Form D
ACFTAASEAN–China · CO Form E
AKFTAASEAN–Korea · CO Form AK
AANZFTAASEAN–Australia–New Zealand · CO Form AANZ
  • ASEAN: key = 10 members. CLMV (Cambodia, Lao PDR, Myanmar, Vietnam) = the four that joined in 1995–1999 = "newer AMS" (#21, #42).
  • CEPT = Common Effective Preferential Tariff. ATIGA concession needs: in the Inclusion List of both countries, Form D, 40% ASEAN content. Not needed: BOI certification.
  • ATIGA Art. 24 highly sensitive products: rice and sugar.
  • ACFTA ag products Ch. 1–8 before effectivity: all at 1% (per key).
  • GSP: developed countries give developing countries preferential rates. PH → EU uses GSP.
  • MFN = Most Favored Nation: an advantage given to one WTO member goes to all.
  • APEC: founded 1989, 21 member economies. BIMP-EAGA in PH: Mindanao & Palawan.
  • ITA Quad: USA, Canada, Japan, EU.
  • ASEAN Single Window: electronic exchange of trade documents among AMS.

9. WCO, WTO & Kyoto

  • WCO was formerly the Customs Cooperation Council (not the WTO).
  • Revised Kyoto Convention: blueprint for modern, efficient customs procedures; PH is a contracting party. Standard = must implement; Transitional = may implement later.
  • RKC principal procedures include home use, transit, temporary admission, warehousing. Not one: re-exportation without entry/customs control.
  • Non-tariff barriers: quotas, import licensing, sanitary rules, prohibitions.
  • Single Window: one entry point for all import/export regulatory requirements.

10. Trade remedies

Anti-dumpingExport price < normal value + material injury. Normal value = comparable price in the exporting country. Material injury = not inconsequential or negligible.
CountervailingOffsets foreign government subsidies. Subsidy = financial contribution by a government/public body conferring a benefit. Specificity = limited to certain enterprises/industries.
SafeguardsRA 8800 (Safeguard Measures Act). Increased imports causing serious injury (significant overall impairment). Element: import surges. TC investigates.
Global vs specialGlobal = imports from all sources. Special = agricultural products under RA 8800. Key #245 says "agricultural and industrial".

Per key: special safeguard requests go to the TC Chairperson (#9) and the President decides (#247). Under RA 8800 it's the DA Secretary for special safeguard requests and the DTI or DA Secretary for decisions. See watch-outs.

3. Demand & inventory

  • Demand planning: forecast future demand using historical data; cuts cost by optimizing production schedules.
  • Inventory planning: balance meeting demand vs minimizing holding costs.
  • WIP = goods still in production. Reorder point = level to place a new order. ABC analysis = by importance.
  • Capital cost = opportunity cost of capital. Obsolescence = cost of holding outdated stock.
  • Inventory turnover: measures efficiency of inventory movement. Higher = efficient, lower holding costs (inverse relationship). Low turnover = slow-moving or obsolete stock.
  • Per key: reducing inventory decreases working capital, improves cash flow, payment terms with suppliers and profit margins.
  • Red flags: frequent stockouts/backorders (poor management), excess stock (obsolescence), poor system integration (delays, errors), long lead times (slow response).
  • Stockouts → lost sales. Accurate valuation → compliance with accounting standards. Sustainable practices → better brand perception.

Ethics scenarios (#361–380)

  • Relationship with a bidder or affiliate → disclose and recuse/manage the conflict.
  • Supplier gift during a bid → follow gift policy, disclose, stay impartial.
  • Lowest price but poor quality → evaluate total cost of ownership.
  • Unusually low bid missing services → normalize bids, clarify scope.
  • Splitting orders to dodge approval = circumvention of procurement controls.
  • Advance payment → contractual safeguards, due diligence, security.
  • Supplier scorecard, balanced view (#377): quality, delivery, cost, responsiveness, compliance, improvement.
  • Fewer suppliers → manage greater dependency risk (#375), but it does not "significantly maximize risk" (#217 = False). Confidential bid info → never share.

5. Production & facilities

  • Production planning: balance resources for efficient manufacturing. MPS sets quantities & timing per finished item. Capacity planning finds constraints. Scheduling assigns tasks to times & resources.
  • Production floor management: coordinate & control shop-floor activity; continuous efficiency improvement.
  • Facilities management: space planning (optimal use + future needs), building maintenance (routine repairs), security & safety (access control, CCTV, emergency plans), sustainability (lower environmental impact), technology integration.

6. Warehousing

  • Warehousing bridges the time gap between production and consumption. Needs synchronization with supply chain objectives.
  • Private = own stock of manufacturers/traders. Public = needs government regulation & licensing. Bonded = imported goods stored before paying import duty.
  • Functions: movement (receiving incl. temporary quarantine storage, cross-docking), storage (semi-permanent = buffer/safety stock), information transfer (timely, accurate).
  • Cross-docking: goods go straight from inbound to outbound; reduces cost, improves service. Per key also: combining stock from many origins into a customer assortment (#181).
  • Spot-stocking: stock near markets for peak season. Full line stocking: year-round use. VAS enhances customer value.
  • Trend: JIT changed traditional warehouse functions; today's warehouses handle more diverse products, incl. offshoring activities (#147, #148).
  • Sizing: project total volume; add 10% extra space. Ideal location: near highways, rail, airports, seaports.
  • Hoover: market positioned = nearest the final customer. Von Thünen: minimize total transportation costs.
  • Layout: maximize space, equipment & labor use. U-shaped flow popular in retail, suits cross-docking. Factor: similarity of products. Cubic use: vertical handling devices. Cost of moving goods in and out = handling (#158).

8. Warehouse KPIs, systems & safety

  • SMART: Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Timely.
  • Productivity measures: labor, space, equipment utilization. Units picked per hour = productivity.
  • Area utilization = (space used × 100) ÷ space available.
  • Dock-to-stock time: vehicle arrival → stock visible in the system.
  • Balanced scorecard (Kaplan & Norton): finance, customer, internal processes, staff development.
  • Financial side: raising funds & deploying resources; working capital cycle = integrated financial flows; maximize circulation velocity; SCM profit via total cost & investment optimization.
  • Safety: forklift accidents mainly struck by a moving vehicle (key, NIOSH); anti-slip mats; inspect working-at-height equipment; random tests on loadings against theft.

9. Bonded warehouse controls (#341–360)

  • Releasing bonded goods without customs authority = unauthorized removal of goods under customs control.
  • Stock variance → secure goods, investigate, reconcile, notify authorities where required.
  • Unexplained shortages → independent reconciliation of customs records, WMS and physical stock.
  • Different customs statuses → clear segregation and status controls.
  • Can't link a withdrawal to its entry → weak traceability.
  • Received but not inspected → quarantine status in the WMS. Damaged seals → controlled exception handling.
  • Count without shutting down → cycle counting. RFID still needs disciplined processes.
  • Recall → lot/batch IDs linked to receipt, location, movement and dispatch.

13. Port, container & resilience scenarios

  • Rule for scenario items: pick the complete, controlled, documented process, never the shortcut.
  • Conflicting updates → shared visibility platform. Typhoon → pre-assessed alternative port. Single source → qualify more suppliers.
  • Reduce landed cost → look at procurement, freight, insurance, duties, storage, delay & handling together.
  • Resilience measure: time to restore service levels. Bullwhip from promos → share POS data.
  • Outsourced transport → SLAs, KPIs, escalation. Unreliable lead times → OTIF.
  • Delivery order ≠ customs release. Missing VGM → obtain & transmit verified gross mass. Seal mismatch → documented investigation.
  • Reefer excursion → preserve data, assess, notify, follow claims. Dangerous goods → safety, segregation, compliance.
  • Truck turnaround KPI → gate-in to gate-out time. Congestion → appointments & yard visibility.
  • Port community system → less duplication, shared trade data. Not enough cargo for FCL → LCL consolidation.
  • Transport KPI pair: on-time delivery rate + cost per shipment. Late-arrival forecasts → predictive analytics. Lost GPS → exception protocols.
